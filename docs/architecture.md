# Where the authority lives

Threshold locks the arrival record before a disagreement exists. Both parties accept the same tenancy terms and item inventory. Arrival photos become immutable commitments before deposit funding. Departure photos use the same item identifiers. No application preview becomes a verdict.

The registry owns parties, condition clauses, item definitions, evidence commitments and network timestamps. A contract timestamp proves when a commitment was submitted. A camera timestamp does not prove when a photo was taken. Both parties must accept the arrival record. Replacement prices come from published references attached to each item, bounded by a jointly accepted replacement ceiling. HUD Appendix 5D supplies useful life as a neutral depreciation reference. It is a sample schedule, not a universal legal mandate.

The court fetches committed bytes, verifies SHA256, and independently evaluates each pair on every validator. Classification, deduction band, item identity and new damage count match exactly. Reasons may differ. Dates and amounts are evaluated in a deterministic sandbox. Ambiguity becomes UNDETERMINED and freezes release pending appeal. A failed network consensus does not fabricate a verdict; the original funds remain held and the protocol receipt stays available for review.

The escrow receives actual native value. A recorded amount never substitutes for funding. StudioNet native balances are simulated by the network, and the interface identifies them as Studio credits. This deployment does not hold fiat money. Escrow reserves are separate from the tenant deposit. A penalty can only spend a funded landlord reserve, never another tenancy's deposit. A punitive finding requires an upheld tenant appeal and bad faith, not simply a missed deadline.

Privy owns email authentication and embedded account access. The app hides wallet mechanics and submits authorized actions with the user's account. The deployment key never becomes a shared tenant signing key. The backend verifies Privy sessions, checks tenancy membership, persists a non authoritative Neon index, and serves immutable evidence objects. Pending submissions remain pending until an execution successful network receipt and contract read confirm them.

Original images never go on chain. Evidence URLs are capability links: validators need access to the bytes, so these links and the photos are not private to chain observers. The capture flow requires people and documents to be excluded, allows retakes and strips metadata before commitment. Users review the final image before upload. A local MediaPipe detector checks for faces before upload. Detection can miss faces; documents and screens require explicit human review.

The first legal profile is ordinary California residential tenancy governed by Civil Code 1950.5, without the small landlord deposit exception. Local exceptions and other rental classes are not silently mapped to California. The law reference, profile scope and supported checks are visible. Statutory checks include the deposit ceiling, a recorded itemised notice within 21 calendar days, and required evidence. The network record proves notice registration, not postal delivery. Both parties agree that the app record serves as their itemised condition notice. Complex disputes and missing authoritative references route to appeal.
